The Morning Barrel

Sunday, September 6, 2026 — Houston

Texas approves $14B transmission superhighway to move Permian power to grid.

WTI jumped to $91.48 and Brent to $96.02, a sharp move driven by tightening supply signals including Enbridge's Line 5 outage (which forced Keyera to cut 2026 guidance) and elevated refining crack spreads that EIA flagged as pushing pump prices higher. Crude stocks drew down 4.45 million barrels while gas storage keeps building, up 30 Bcf to 3,214 Bcf. ERCOT load sits near 65 GW with gas covering 54% of generation and wind at 22%, consistent with the record-high summer demand pattern utilities have flagged all season.

What matters today

  • Texas regulators approved a $14 billion Permian Basin transmission buildout, a multi-year project to bring reliable grid power to oilfield operations and data centers that have relied on behind-the-meter generation.
  • Sempra filed permits to add 27 mtpa of LNG capacity at Port Arthur, Texas, one of the largest single expansion proposals yet on the Gulf Coast.
  • Cheniere said it has substantially completed its Texas LNG (Corpus Christi) expansion project, adding to the wave of Gulf Coast liquefaction capacity coming online.
  • Equinor received its first US Gulf Coast LNG cargo, a step the company says puts it on track to double its global LNG portfolio by 2030.
  • Construction started on Blue Point One, a low-carbon ammonia plant in Modeste, Louisiana backed by CF Industries (40%), JERA (35%) and Mitsui (25%).

Threads worth following

  • Gulf Coast LNG capacity is expanding faster than Asian offtake commitments (China still isn't buying US cargoes), raising the question of who absorbs the next wave of supply from Sempra, Cheniere, and Equinor.
  • ERCOT's transmission investment and data-center demand growth are forcing a rethink of how Texas grid infrastructure keeps pace with both oilfield electrification and AI-driven load.
  • BLM's proposal to drop project-by-project environmental review for NPR-A signals a broader federal push to streamline permitting that could extend to other onshore and offshore leasing decisions.
