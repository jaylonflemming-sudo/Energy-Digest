Wednesday, September 16, 2026 — Houston

Cheniere completes Corpus Christi Stage 3, making it the second-largest US LNG export facility.

Crude jumped over $3/bbl on both benchmarks after a Saudi pipeline shutdown and an IEA report showing Ukrainian drone strikes are degrading Russian refining capacity, both tightening global supply and product markets. Henry Hub eased slightly as storage builds continue ahead of winter, with Lower 48 stocks up 40 Bcf to 3,254 Bcf. ERCOT's midday mix was solar-heavy at 39% against 70.5 GW of load, with gas providing 36% and wind lagging at just 7%.

What matters today

  • Cheniere took custody of the seventh and final LNG train at Corpus Christi Stage 3 on August 28, making the Texas facility the country's second-largest LNG export terminal.
  • Enbridge is acquiring Tallgrass Energy's crude oil transportation business for $2.55 billion, including a 75% stake in the 1,050-mile Pony Express Pipeline linking DJ Basin and Bakken production to Cushing.
  • S&P Global and the IEA both flag deepening refined product tightness, driven by the Saudi pipeline outage and sustained Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian refineries.
  • Cipher Digital secured conditional ERCOT interconnection status for 3.2 GW of Texas capacity, underscoring the scale of crypto and data-center load now queuing for grid access.
  • The Texas General Land Office announced it will explore developing power plants on state-owned land, a new avenue for adding generation capacity in ERCOT.
  • RBN's Q2 2026 review finds E&Ps shifting capital back toward shareholder returns over reinvestment now that balance sheets have largely been repaired.

Threads worth following

  • Watch how fast Corpus Christi Stage 3 volumes ramp into winter feedgas demand (already at 18.9 Bcf/d) and which buyers — Thailand's PTT, China's expanded contracts — absorb the new capacity.
  • ERCOT's queue of gigawatt-scale data center and crypto loads (like Cipher's 3.2 GW) is becoming a bigger grid-planning question than renewables intermittency alone.
  • Sustained Russian refining disruption from drone strikes plus Saudi pipeline problems could keep global product cracks elevated longer than a typical short-term supply scare.
