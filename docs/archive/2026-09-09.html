Wednesday, September 9, 2026 — Houston

Crude spikes over $4-6/bbl as Venezuela supply outlook and OPEC+ steady quotas collide

WTI jumped to $91.48 and Brent to $96.02, a sharp move tied to renewed uncertainty over Venezuelan output following RBN's read on the sanctions-easing production recovery, plus OPEC+ holding October quotas flat while it debates 2027 allocations. Crude stocks drew down 4.45 million bbl to 424.5 million, and gas storage added 30 Bcf to 3,214 Bcf, comfortably tracking toward the winter injection target. Henry Hub sat flat at $2.90 even as LNG feedgas volumes push toward winter-peak levels, underscoring how disconnected domestic gas pricing has become from export demand.

What matters today

  • Enbridge named Colin Gruending... correction, named Harradence to succeed Ebel as president and CEO, a leadership transition at one of North America's largest pipeline operators.
  • Eni Cyprus awarded McDermott the contract for the Cronos offshore gas development, advancing one of the Eastern Mediterranean's key gas projects.
  • ExxonMobil is taking over as operator of the Papua LNG project from TotalEnergies, a shift in control over a major new PNG export development.
  • Williams closed its $5.5 billion acquisition of Momentum Midstream, expanding its Gulf Coast gathering and processing footprint ahead of rising LNG feedgas demand.
  • DOE finalized a $1.9 billion loan to restart the shuttered Duane Arnold nuclear plant in Iowa, the third U.S. nuclear plant now targeted for a comeback amid data-center-driven power demand.

Threads worth following

  • Venezuela's production and export recovery under eased U.S. sanctions is still fragile, but if it holds it reshapes medium-term heavy crude supply and Gulf Coast refinery sourcing.
  • ERCOT's Batch Zero large-load classifications and the conditional 2 GW Sweetwater Hub approval show how data center and crypto demand is now driving formal grid interconnection processes, not just load forecasts.
  • The Duane Arnold restart adds to a pattern of federally financed nuclear revivals aimed squarely at data center power demand, worth tracking against the two other U.S. restart efforts already underway.
