What matters today

  • Sempra filed permits for a 27 mtpa LNG expansion at Port Arthur, Texas, one of the largest single additions proposed on the Gulf Coast to date.
  • Williams closed its $5.5 billion acquisition expanding its Haynesville shale gas holdings.
  • New pipeline capacity out of the Permian is set to flip the basin from chronic gas takeaway constraints to an outbound capacity glut, per RBN Energy analysis, which could pressure Waha prices from the other direction.
  • Chevron is targeting a $7 billion investment in Venezuela, signaling a bigger bet on reentering that country's production despite sanctions uncertainty.
  • ERCOT will complete its audit of Texas data center power demand by December, while AEP secured a federal loan to fund grid upgrades tied to that same load growth.

Threads worth following

  • Permian gas pipeline overbuild: if outbound capacity now exceeds supply, Waha could swing from negative prices to a genuine bottleneck of demand, reshaping producer economics across the basin.
  • Gulf Coast LNG buildout keeps growing (Sempra's Port Arthur filing on top of existing capacity) — watch how much of this actually reaches FID versus stalls on permitting or offtake.
  • Texas grid planning for data centers is moving from talk to paperwork (ERCOT audit, AEP federal loan), which will shape how much new gas and renewable capacity gets built to serve that load through the 2030s.
