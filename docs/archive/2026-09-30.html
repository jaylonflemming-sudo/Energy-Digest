What matters today

  • LNG Canada's equity partners (Shell, Petronas, PetroChina, Mitsubishi, Kogas) sanctioned a Phase 2 expansion on September 29 that will double the Kitimat facility's nameplate output, with first gas expected early next decade but no firm completion date disclosed.
  • Cheniere signed a 22-year LNG supply deal with Petrobras, extending Gulf Coast export commitments deep into the 2040s.
  • Comstock Resources and SOCAR signed a $1.65 billion framework deal covering Haynesville shale development, tying Azerbaijan's state oil company directly to US gas supply feeding Gulf Coast LNG.
  • Fervo Energy's Cape Station in Utah began selling power to the grid, the first next-generation geothermal plant to reach utility scale anywhere in the world.
  • Permian natural gas output is running near record highs this month as new takeaway from the Hugh Brinson pipeline, Blackcomb and the Gulf Coast Express expansion comes online, part of roughly 11 Bcf/d of new basin egress scheduled through 2029.

Threads worth following

  • Whether the wave of new Permian gas pipelines (11 Bcf/d by 2029) actually shifts basin economics toward gas-directed drilling, or associated gas from oil wells keeps dominating supply growth regardless.
  • A looming Gulf Coast gas storage shortfall as LNG export capacity scales up faster than new storage gets built — watch who commits capital first, since site economics now depend heavily on what exporters are willing to pay.
  • The NRC's proposed 'wholesale revision' of reactor licensing rules could materially speed up advanced reactor and SMR permitting, a prerequisite for the nuclear capacity many data-center deals are counting on.
