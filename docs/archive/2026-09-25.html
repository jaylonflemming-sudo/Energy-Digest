What matters today

  • Fervo Energy reached first power at its flagship Cape Station enhanced geothermal project in Utah, a milestone for commercial-scale geothermal as a firm, always-on power source.
  • DOE announced $1.9 billion for advanced transmission technologies (grid-enhancing tech, reconductoring, dynamic line rating) aimed at squeezing more capacity out of congested US grids without new lines.
  • A Deployable Energy microreactor achieved criticality at Idaho National Laboratory, another advanced-reactor demonstration moving from paper to hardware.
  • EOG Resources named a new CFO, Hibbard, succeeding Janssen, as the Permian/Eagle Ford producer heads into 2027 capital planning.
  • Power Electronics is building its largest US manufacturing campus for power conversion equipment in Houston, adding domestic capacity for inverters and grid hardware tied to renewables and data center buildout.
  • US Energy Secretary said US LNG exports are on pace to exceed 120 million tonnes in 2026, underscoring continued Gulf Coast export capacity ramp.

Threads worth following

  • ERCOT's summer performance and projected 2030s demand surge from data centers is colliding with TCEQ's data center permit freeze — watch whether that freeze slows load growth or just shifts it to other states.
  • The proposed 765-kV transmission line is drawing organized political pushback in Texas, a test case for whether high-voltage buildout can keep pace with ERCOT's load growth amid local opposition.
  • DOE's $1.9B grid tech push contrasts with the frozen $7B Solar for All program now facing a legal reversal — two policy tracks moving in opposite directions on federal clean-energy support.
