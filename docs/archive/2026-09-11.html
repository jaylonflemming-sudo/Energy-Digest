Friday, September 11, 2026 — Houston

Oil surges past $100 WTI as Middle East supply risks escalate sharply

WTI jumped $3.05 to $97.26 and Brent $3.39 to $109.51 on intensifying Middle East supply disruption fears, though EIA still projects Brent easing back toward $90 in the second half of 2026, suggesting the market sees this as a risk premium rather than a structural shift. Henry Hub slipped to $2.81 as storage built another 40 Bcf to 3,254 Bcf, keeping gas comfortably supplied heading into fall. Crude stocks drew a modest 391 kbbl, holding near 424 million barrels.

What matters today

  • Summit Midstream is moving ahead with a 900 MMcf/d expansion of its Double E Pipeline out of the Permian after locking in about 550 MMcf/d of long-term shipper commitments.
  • Black Bayou unveiled a $1.6 billion natural gas storage expansion in Louisiana, adding to the wave of Gulf Coast midstream buildout tied to export demand.
  • Shell and partners signed an agreement for offshore blocks in Angola, extending the company's deepwater West Africa portfolio.
  • Argent LNG and CB&I signed an MoU as the Gulf Coast LNG development pipeline continues to expand alongside export capacity growth.
  • ERCOT's interconnection queue shows accelerating data center and crypto-mining demand: Core Scientific holds conditional approvals covering up to 1,402 MW, Ionic Digital got a provisional 466 MW Base Load classification, and Sangha's Texas campus secured Base Load status for 110.4 MW.
  • EIA forecasts U.S. crude production will hit a record 13.8 million b/d in 2026, topping last year's 13.7 million b/d mark, even as executives at the Barclays conference debate how aggressively to reinvest rising cash flows.

Threads worth following

  • Watch whether the Middle East-driven price spike holds or fades toward EIA's $90 Brent H2 forecast — that gap will shape E&P capital discipline decisions industry-wide.
  • ERCOT's growing stack of large-load approvals for data centers and crypto miners (Core Scientific, Ionic Digital, Sangha) is a preview of the grid capacity strain regulators expect through the 2030s.
  • The Trump administration's grid cybersecurity emergency order is creating uncertainty for battery storage deployment just as China's dominance in battery supply chains draws more scrutiny.
