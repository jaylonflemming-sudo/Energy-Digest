What matters today

  • Williams agreed to buy Momentum Midstream for $5.5 billion, consolidating gas gathering and processing assets.
  • Plains All American is acquiring Silver Creek's integrated crude gathering system in the Powder River Basin for $585 million.
  • Governor Abbott ordered TCEQ to halt all environmental permits for data centers until ERCOT and the Texas Water Development Board complete grid and water-supply audits.
  • North Carolina regulators rejected Duke Energy's proposal to build a new gas plant, an unusual pushback from a panel that typically defers to the utility.
  • Quaise Energy was selected for up to $25 million in DOE funding to advance what it calls the world's first commercial superhot geothermal power plant.

Threads worth following

  • Texas's data center permit freeze is a direct response to fears that AI-driven load growth could outpace ERCOT capacity and water supplies — the audit outcome will shape how fast new campuses get built statewide.
  • Midstream M&A is accelerating (Williams/Momentum at $5.5B, Plains/Silver Creek at $585M), signaling operators are racing to lock down gathering and processing footprints in gas-heavy basins before the next wave of LNG feedgas demand hits.
  • Utility regulators pushing back on new gas plants (Duke in NC) while approving large solar-plus-storage projects (Oak Run in Ohio) suggests state commissions are getting more skeptical of gas buildout even as data center demand grows — worth watching whether this spreads to other states.
