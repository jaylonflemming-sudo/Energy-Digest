The Morning Barrel

Thursday, September 17, 2026 — Houston

Crude surges to $130 Brent as VLCC rates hit record highs amid Venezuela supply turmoil.

Brent jumped $9.55 to $130.80 and WTI $4.60 to $107.02, driven by record VLCC freight rates that are straining crude logistics and by fresh uncertainty over Venezuelan supply after Washington froze Citgo's board to protect a pending $5.9 billion sale. Crude stocks drew down 640 kbbl to 423.4 million barrels while gas storage built 44 Bcf to 3,298 Bcf, comfortably above the five-year average heading into fall. ERCOT load sat at 70,407 MW with solar the single largest fuel source at 38%, ahead of gas at 36%, wind at 9% and nuclear at 6%.

What matters today

  • The US government froze Citgo's board to protect a pending $5.9 billion acquisition, adding a new layer of uncertainty to Venezuela's oil sector as Heeney Capital moves to operate a Venezuelan gold mine and Continental seeks entry into a Venezuelan oil project.
  • Chord Energy is selling $550 million of Marcellus Shale assets to concentrate capital in the Williston Basin, part of a broader Q2 2026 trend of E&Ps favoring shareholder returns over new drilling as balance sheets strengthen.
  • Wolf Midstream sanctioned a Phase Three expansion of its NGL North system in Alberta, adding takeaway capacity as Canadian gas producers push more volumes toward US Gulf Coast LNG terminals.
  • Aker BP reported a gas discovery near the Duva field in the North Sea, while Azule Energy awarded a subsea contract for the West Hub Tails development offshore Angola.
  • A US Gulf Coast LNG project moved forward with plans that would nearly double its export capacity, underscoring continued momentum in Gulf Coast liquefaction buildout despite tighter global shipping markets.

Threads worth following

  • Venezuela's oil and mining sector is in flux with Citgo's ownership frozen and new US and foreign entrants circling upstream assets — a shift worth tracking for its effect on medium-sour crude supply into Gulf Coast refineries.
  • E&Ps are pivoting decisively toward shareholder payouts rather than reinvestment even as prices spike, a capital discipline trend that could cap US production growth response to the current rally.
  • ERCOT's board reversed a multimillion-dollar CEO bonus deal after backlash, right as the same group is meeting to plan grid capacity for a wave of AI and data center load — governance friction at a moment of critical planning decisions.
