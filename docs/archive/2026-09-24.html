What matters today

  • Governor Abbott tightened a pause on new ERCOT interconnection permits for data centers, following an earlier water-use directive, as the grid operator projects a major demand surge into the 2030s from AI and data center growth.
  • Shell-led LNG Canada is set to approve an expansion of its British Columbia export terminal, adding to North American LNG capacity beyond the current 14 mtpa first phase.
  • PTTEP took final investment decision on a new offshore development in the Gulf of Thailand, adding to a string of Asian upstream sanctions this week alongside OQEP's Bisat field expansion contract in Oman.
  • DOE will spend $2 billion on grid-enhancing technologies to squeeze more capacity from the aging transmission network, part of the same push that sent nearly $45 million to Houston-area geothermal companies.
  • X-energy's first-of-a-kind small modular reactor project is advancing with new federal funding as the administration leans further into nuclear as a data-center power solution.

Threads worth following

  • Texas's data center permitting freeze is a direct test of whether ERCOT's projected 2030s demand surge is real capacity planning or overbuilt speculation — the audit's outcome will shape where hyperscalers site next.
  • China isn't taking US LNG cargoes but keeps trading and investing around the edges, a signal that global LNG flows are being reshaped by geopolitics rather than pure economics as US export capacity heads toward 120 million tonnes in 2026.
  • Diesel cracks holding above $100/bbl for two straight weeks raises the stakes on whether Chinese refiners, sitting on spare capacity, actually start exporting product to relieve the global crunch.
