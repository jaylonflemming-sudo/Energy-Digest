Brent jumps to $130.80 as Iran conflict fallout hits tanker rates and crude flows

Brent's $9.55 surge and WTI's $4.60 gain track escalating disruption risk tied to the Iran conflict, with VLCC freight rates hitting record highs as owners reprice war risk and reroute around chokepoints. Henry Hub ticked up to $2.97 despite a hefty 44-Bcf storage build that keeps the Lower 48 at 3,298 Bcf, comfortably above five-year norms heading into fall. ERCOT is running a heavily solar-weighted midday mix (40% solar, 36% gas) on 67 GW of load, typical for mid-September before evening ramps shift the burden to gas.

What matters today

  • Williams agreed to buy Momentum Midstream for $5.5 billion, expanding its gas gathering and processing footprint as basin M&A consolidates.
  • Enbridge launched a non-binding open season for the 2-Bcf/d West Texas Express pipeline to move Permian gas from Waha to El Paso, aimed at Texas power demand growth.
  • ExxonMobil advanced its Gulf Coast CCS network with approval of the Rose project and startup of Nucor's capture unit, adding an industrial anchor customer to the pipeline system.
  • Shell-led LNG Canada could sanction a Phase 2 expansion by early October, roughly doubling the project's export capacity if approved.
  • Mazama Energy raised $135 million to advance superhot-rock geothermal drilling near Oregon's Newberry Volcano, betting on deeper, hotter wells for baseload clean power.

Threads worth following

  • Watch whether Iran-related tanker rate spikes and Brent-WTI spread widening persist — sustained disruption would reshape US export economics and refinery crude sourcing.
  • LNG Canada's Phase 2 decision, if it lands in October, would be a major signal on Asian LNG demand confidence and Canada's position against Gulf Coast export growth.
  • The Enbridge West Texas Express open season is another data point on Permian gas takeaway racing to keep pace with Texas power demand — a bottleneck that's been recurring all year.
