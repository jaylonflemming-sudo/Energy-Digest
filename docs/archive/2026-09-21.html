Brent surges past $130 on geopolitical risk as Williams buys Momentum Midstream for $5.5B

Brent's $9.55 jump to $130.80 and WTI's matching surge to $107 reflect a geopolitical risk premium building into the market, likely tied to Venezuela-related supply uncertainty given Halliburton's re-entry pacts there this week. What matters today

  • Williams agreed to buy Momentum Midstream for $5.5 billion, expanding its gas gathering and processing footprint as Permian and other basin volumes grow.
  • Halliburton signed energy pacts with Venezuela's Eneva and WESCA, signaling US oilfield services testing renewed access to Venezuelan crude assets.
  • Wood Mackenzie warned that Asian shipyards' dominance in LNG carrier construction could bottleneck global export capacity buildouts over the next decade.
  • RBN Energy argues new pipeline takeaway capacity has effectively ended the era of negative Permian natural gas prices, a persistent problem tied to associated-gas oversupply.
  • ERCOT's leadership is in turmoil: the board's CEO contract is paused and Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick is demanding reversal of a 30% board pay raise, even as the grid operator runs a data center audit and forecasts a major demand surge into the 2030s.

Threads worth following

  • Watch whether the Venezuela opening (Halliburton, Eneva, WESCA deals) expands to other US majors and whether it's tied to sanctions relief that could eventually add barrels to a tight market.
  • ERCOT's governance fight is happening at the exact moment Texas needs credible planning for data-center-driven load growth — a leadership vacuum here has real reliability stakes.
  • The Williams-Momentum deal is part of a broader midstream consolidation wave; watch for more M&A as gatherers position for Permian and Haynesville volume growth now that pipeline bottlenecks are easing.
