What matters today

  • Williams agreed to buy Momentum Midstream for $5.5 billion, consolidating gas gathering and processing assets tied to Haynesville production.
  • TotalEnergies took final investment decision on full-field development of the Absheron gas condensate field offshore Azerbaijan.
  • Comstock Resources and SOCAR advanced a $1.65 billion framework agreement to develop Haynesville shale assets together.
  • Oil & Gas Journal reports natural gas pipeline project proposals have roughly tripled even as per-mile construction costs are falling, signaling a wave of new interstate capacity ahead of LNG feedgas demand.
  • Urban Grid rebranded as Panthera Grid Partners, now carrying a development pipeline of more than 10 GW of solar, wind, and storage across the US.
  • Kimmeridge warns that US shale oil reserve replacement is weakening even as associated gas output stays abundant, a signal for capital discipline in the Permian and Eagle Ford.

Threads worth following

  • Watch whether Washington moves from talk to action on limiting US diesel exports — RBN and others flag the policy carries real risk of raising domestic gasoline and crude prices while doing little to fix the underlying refining capacity shortfall.
  • ERCOT's own forecasts for data center-driven demand growth into the 2030s are colliding with permitting bottlenecks in Congress; the stalled Senate permitting bill will shape how fast new gas, transmission, and storage capacity can actually get built in Texas.
  • Midstream consolidation (Williams-Momentum, Comstock-SOCAR) is accelerating as Haynesville gas positions itself as the primary feedstock for Gulf Coast LNG expansion, worth tracking for who controls gathering and takeaway capacity.
